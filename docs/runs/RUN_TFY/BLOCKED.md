# RUN ΤΦΥ — what is blocked, and what is in the build instead

Every entry names the thing this app cannot do, the reason, and **the workaround that shipped**. A
blocker with no workaround is not allowed in this run (R1), and there are none.

---

## 1. `GET v1/symbols` does not exist (F1)

**What the brief asks for.** A universe at `/v1/symbols` carrying
`{id, base, quote, venue, type, displayNameEn, displayNameFa, logoBase, logoQuote, tickSize, minQty, status, turnover24h}`.

**What answers today**, checked from this container on 2026-09-17:

| Address | Answer |
|---|---|
| `https://coineprofx.com/v1/symbols` | `200`, and it is **the web app's own HTML** — the SPA's catch-all route, not an address |
| `https://coineprofx.com/api/v1/symbols` | `404` |
| `https://coineprofx.com/ws/snapshot` | `200`, **19 symbols**: the two metals, ten currency pairs, four indices and oil |
| `https://tradeyar.trade-future.ir/v1/symbols` | `307` to `/login` — the route needs a session, and this container has none |

So the forex platform serves a nineteen-symbol universe and no listing endpoint at all, and the
crypto platform's answer cannot be read from here.

**What shipped.**

* `NetworkSymbolUniverseGateway` is written **to the brief's shape**, field for field, and is wired
  into both platforms. The day the address exists the app reads it with no release.
* It falls through — `v1/symbols`, then the snapshot, then the bundled table — and the fall-through
  is the right shape even with the endpoint live: a venue that is slow, rate-limited or mid-deploy
  must not empty the markets tab.
* `BundledUniverse` carries **300 crypto pairs by market capitalisation plus the 49 non-crypto
  markets this app has flags or marks for**, generated by `scripts/design/build-symbol-universe.py`.
  That is what a reader on CoinePro-FX now sees instead of nineteen rows.

**What the owner must provide.** `GET v1/symbols` on either backend, in the brief's shape. The two
fields that cannot be worked around from here are **`turnover24h`** — which is what ranks a markets
list and what the F2 filter's «over $10M» reads — and **`status`**, which is how a delisted market
says so instead of simply having no price. Everything else the client derives.

## 2. Nothing serves an entitlement (F8)

**What the brief asks for.** `entitlements.all`, server-fed, with a local default of `true`.

**What answers today.** Neither backend has an entitlements route. What they *do* have is walls of
their own: the academy sends `locked` and a `lockReason` per lesson, and the signal list answers a
reader without membership with a 403.

**What shipped.** `FeatureFlags.allUnlocked` is the switch and `Entitlements` is what reads it,
local, defaulting to true, with the shape a server-fed value would slot into unchanged. Every
client-side wall reads it.

**What it cannot open, and what it does instead.** A lock the **server** declared is not ours to
lift. Rather than painting a door shut on a field that may be a release out of date,
`Entitlements.attemptsLockedContent` makes the app **try**: a tier-locked academy lesson is live and
tappable, the request goes out, and the wall appears only if the backend actually refuses — with the
server's own copy. A phone-number lock is left alone, because that one the reader can fix in a
screen and a refusal they were not warned about is worse.

**What the owner must provide.** Either an entitlements route, or — simpler, and what this period
actually means — the academy's tiers and the signal history opened server-side too. The client is
already asking.

## 3. The founding-member mark is local (F10)

**What the brief asks for.** «An account created while the switch is on carries a permanent badge»,
with the note that a local flag is acceptable until the endpoint exists.

**What shipped.** `ProfileStore.foundingMember`, written once, the first time the app runs while
`Entitlements.foundingMember` is true, and never written again — so somebody installing after the
period does not get it and somebody who has it does not lose it when the period ends. It is drawn on
the profile, on the reader's own community posts, and on the foot of a shared card.

**The limit, stated where it is felt.** It lives on **this phone**. A reinstall loses it and a second
device never had it — and on the community board it can only ever be drawn on the reader's *own*
posts, because the app knows it about exactly one person. A board where a stranger's badge depended
on which device you read it from would be worse than a board with no badges.

**What the owner must provide.** A field on the account — `founding_member` on whatever the profile
route is — set server-side at registration while the period is open. One boolean, and the three
places it is drawn stop being local the day it arrives.

## 5. Three of the four pulse figures, and the watchlist's market cap (U3, U6)

**What the brief asks for.** A row at the top of the markets surface reading «ارزش کل بازار · حجم ۲۴
ساعت · سهم بیت‌کوین · شاخص ترس و طمع», with «any value that cannot be computed rendered as «—» and
the missing input named here». And a watchlist row carrying a market capitalisation beside the
ticker.

**What shipped.** The row, with one real figure and three dashes, and every cell — dash or not —
opening a sentence that says what it is measured over or what it would need. The three are not
placeholders and they are not a loading state.

**What each one actually needs, one line each.**

* **ارزش کل بازار** and **سهم بیت‌کوین** need a **circulating supply per asset**. Market cap is price ×
  supply and dominance is one cap over the sum of them, so the two are the same missing field. What
  this app receives per symbol is a last price, a 24-hour change and a turnover; there is no supply
  anywhere on either wire. A capitalisation derived from turnover would carry a name people know and
  mean something else, which is worse than a dash.
* **شاخص ترس و طمع** is a **published index that belongs to somebody else**. Neither backend carries
  it. `MarketMood`'s own note has said since it was written why this app will not compute a number of
  its own and print that name over it: a reader who has seen the real index would compare the two and
  find them disagreeing. What *is* printed, under its own name and never in that cell, is breadth —
  the share of the board that is up today, which this app can measure from the table it already has.
* **حجم ۲۴ ساعت** is computed and is **one venue's book**, not the world's. `MarketPulse.turnoverIsVenueOnly`
  is true whenever the figure exists, and the sheet says so rather than letting it be read as global
  volume.
* The **watchlist's market-cap column** is the first bullet again. It is not drawn at all rather than
  drawn as a column of dashes, because a column every row of which is empty is a column that costs
  width — and width is exactly what that row does not have (see U6's note on the 32 dp).

**What the owner must provide.** Any one of three, in order of how little it costs:

1. A **supply figure per symbol** on the existing symbols route — `circulating_supply` beside
   `tick_size` would be enough for both the cap and the dominance, computed client-side from prices
   this app already holds.
2. A **`/v1/market/global`**-shaped route returning `total_market_cap`, `total_volume_24h` and
   `btc_dominance` as the venue or an aggregator computes them.
3. A **fear-and-greed reading** proxied through either backend — the published one, attributed —
   rather than computed. It is the only one of the four that must not be derived locally.

Each arrives as a non-null field on `MarketPulse` and the row draws it. Nothing else changes: the
four cells are already in their places and the frame `upsilon-pulse-full-phone-fa.png` is what that
day looks like.
